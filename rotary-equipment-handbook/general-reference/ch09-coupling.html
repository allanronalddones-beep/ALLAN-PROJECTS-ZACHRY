9.1What a coupling has to do

Table 9.1-1 — Coupling functions
FunctionDetail
Transmit torqueContinuously at rated load, and survive peak and transient torques
Accommodate misalignmentParallel (offset), angular, and combinations — residual after alignment, plus thermal growth in service
Accommodate axial movementThermal growth, rotor float, thrust bearing clearance
Separate the machinesSpacer gives access to the seal without moving the driver
Tune the torsional systemCoupling stiffness and inertia place the train's torsional natural frequencies
Limit transmitted forceA flexible coupling must not impose large forces and moments on the machine bearings
A coupling does not correct misalignment — it tolerates it. Running misaligned within the coupling's rating still loads the bearings and seals of both machines, and the reaction force grows with the offset. Misalignment is a leading cause of bearing and seal failure. Align properly (Chapter 13); the coupling's capacity is there for thermal movement and residual error, not as an alignment budget.

Disc and diaphragm versus gear

Dry flexible (disc / diaphragm)Gear
WearNone in normal service — no relative slidingTooth wear is continuous; life is finite
Lubrication failureNot applicableLoss of lube causes rapid failure; a common root cause
Axial force generatedPredictable, proportional to axial displacement and element stiffnessUnpredictable — depends on friction at the teeth, which varies with lubrication condition
Failure modeFatigue cracking of discs — usually progressiveTooth wear, then rapid
Overload behaviourDiscs can fail suddenly if overloadedMore tolerant of momentary overload
Torque densityLower for a given diameterHigher
BalanceGood — no clearancesClearances permit eccentricity and unbalance shift
Why dry couplings displaced gear couplings on critical machines. The unpredictable axial force from a gear coupling, which depends on the friction coefficient at the teeth, feeds directly into the thrust bearing. As lubrication degrades the force grows, and thrust bearing failures traceable to coupling lock-up were common. A disc or diaphragm coupling's axial force is a calculable spring rate. API 671 strongly favours non-lubricated designs.

9.3Torque

T[N·m]= 9549⁢P[kW] N[rpm]

In US units:

T[lbf·in]= 63025⁢P[hp] N[rpm]

Worked example. 2200 kW motor at 2980 rpm.

T = 9549 × 2200 / 2980 = 7050 N·m continuous. The coupling must then be selected for this torque multiplied by the applicable service factor (§9.4), and separately checked against the peak transient torque.

Table 9.3-1 — Torque cases to check
CaseSourceRelative magnitude
Normal continuousRated power at rated speed1.0
Maximum continuousEnd-of-curve power, maximum impeller, cold fluid1.1 – 1.3
Motor startingDirect-on-line start, locked rotor and pull-up torque1.5 – 2.5
Motor short circuitTerminal fault or out-of-phase re-closureup to 10+
Synchronous motor startingPulsating torque during run-up, exciting torsional modesOscillating
Reciprocating machineCyclic torque variation each revolutionOscillating, large
The short-circuit case is the one that breaks couplings. An induction motor subjected to a terminal short circuit or an out-of-phase re-closure delivers a transient air-gap torque many times rated — commonly cited at up to around ten times. API 671 requires the coupling to withstand defined transient torques without permanent deformation. Where a train includes a large motor, the electrical fault torque must be obtained from the motor vendor and given to the coupling vendor; it is not derivable from the nameplate.

Treat these as a starting point only. Service factors are a crude proxy for a torsional analysis. For API 671 special-purpose trains the service factor does not replace the torsional study — API 671 requires a minimum continuous rating margin over the driver's rated power, plus demonstrated capability against the specified transient torques. For general-purpose drives, AGMA 9000-series factors are the usual basis, and the vendor's own catalogue factors govern.

9.5Spacer length and hub mounting

Spacer

The spacer sets the distance between shaft ends. Two considerations compete:

Longer spacerShorter spacer
More misalignment capacity for the same angular deflection of each elementLess overhung mass and moment on the shaft ends
Room to withdraw the seal cartridge without moving the driverHigher coupling lateral natural frequency
Easier access for alignment measurementLower cost
API 610 requires a spacer coupling. The minimum spacer length on an API 610 pump is specified so that the mechanical seal and bearing housing can be removed without disturbing the driver or the suction and discharge piping. This is why back-pull-out designs and spacer couplings go together, and why fitting a short coupling "to save space" costs far more in every future seal change.

Hub mounting

Table 9.5-1 — Hub-to-shaft connections
MethodTorque capacityNotes
Straight bore with key, clearance fitLowFretting and backlash. Not for high-speed or reversing duty.
Straight bore with key, interference fitModerateHeated for assembly. Common general purpose.
Tapered bore with key, hydraulic fitHighAPI 671 standard. Precise, repeatable, removable.
Tapered bore, keyless, hydraulic fitHighestNo key means no stress raiser and no unbalance from the keyway. Preferred for high-speed trains.
Flanged / integralHighestBolted to a shaft-end flange; common on gas turbines
Record the hydraulic fit advance. A tapered hydraulic fit is set by driving the hub a specified axial distance up the taper, not by torque or by feel. Under-advance slips in service; over-advance can burst the hub. The advance figure and the measured value belong in the installation record, and the hub must be removed and reset if there is any doubt.

9.6Torsional considerations

The coupling is usually the softest torsional element in a train, which means it largely sets where the torsional natural frequencies fall. That makes it a tuning element as well as a power transmission element.

Table 9.6-1 — Torsional excitation sources
SourceExcitation frequency
Any rotor unbalance or misalignment1× and 2× running speed
Reciprocating compressor / pumpMultiples of running speed set by cylinder count and action
Synchronous motor startSweeping pulsating torque at twice slip frequency, from 2× line down to zero
Variable frequency driveHarmonics of the output frequency and of the switching scheme
Electrical faultsLine frequency and twice line frequency transients
Gear meshTooth passing frequency
Torsional vibration is invisible. Unlike lateral vibration, it produces no casing movement and does not show on standard vibration monitoring. The first indication is frequently a broken shaft, a failed coupling or cracked gear teeth. For trains with reciprocating machines, synchronous motors or VFDs, a torsional analysis is mandatory under API 671 and the relevant machine standard — it is not a discretionary study.
Separation margin. API practice requires torsional natural frequencies to be separated from significant excitation frequencies by a margin, commonly 10 % above and below over the operating speed range. Where separation cannot be achieved, a stress analysis must demonstrate acceptable life. The usual remedies are to change coupling stiffness or inertia — which is why the torsional analysis must be completed before the coupling is finally ordered.

9.7Balance and API 671

Table 9.7-1 — Key API 671 requirements
AreaRequirement in outline
TypeNon-lubricated flexible element designs preferred
RatingContinuous rating with margin over the driver rating; defined capability against transient torques
BalanceComponent balance as standard, with assembly balance where specified; residual unbalance limits tied to the machine's balance quality
InterchangeabilityComponents must be interchangeable without rebalancing where specified
Marriage / match markingAssembly orientation marked so the coupling reassembles as balanced
OverspeedCapability to defined overspeed, with spin testing where specified
GuardsNon-sparking material in hazardous areas; must not be a vibration source
DocumentationTorsional stiffness, mass, inertia (WR²), centre of gravity, axial stiffness — needed for the train's rotordynamic and torsional models
Match marking is not a formality. A balanced coupling assembly is balanced in one particular angular relationship between hub, spacer and flex elements. Reassembling it rotated by one bolt hole reintroduces unbalance. Every component carries a match mark; alignment of those marks is a hold point in reassembly.

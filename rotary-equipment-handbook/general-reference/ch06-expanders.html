6  Expander Selection

Recovering shaft work from a pressure let-down, at cryogenic and at high temperature.

6.1Principle

An expander is a compressor run backwards. Gas at high pressure does work on a rotor as it expands, the rotor drives a load, and the gas leaves colder than a throttling valve would have left it. Two distinct benefits follow, and a given application usually wants one of them more than the other.

Table 6.1-1 — What the expander is for
ObjectiveServiceTemperature regime
Refrigeration — produce coldGas processing, NGL recovery, air separation, LNG liquefaction, ethyleneCryogenic, down to −150 °C and below
Power recovery — produce shaft workFCC flue gas, nitric acid tail gas, blast furnace top gas, natural gas pressure let-downHot, 350 to 700 °C — or ambient
Isenthalpic versus isentropic. A Joule-Thomson valve expands at constant enthalpy and extracts no work. An expander approaches constant entropy and extracts work, so for the same pressure drop it produces substantially more cooling — typically two to three times the temperature drop of a JT valve, and shaft power as well. That is the entire economic case.

6.2Expansion calculations

Isentropic enthalpy drop

Δhs= cp⁢T1 [1− (p2p1) k−1k ]
Δhs
isentropic enthalpy drop, kJ/kg
cp
specific heat at constant pressure, kJ/(kg·K)
T1
inlet temperature, K (absolute)
p1, p2
inlet and outlet pressure, absolute
k
specific heat ratio

Recovered power

P[kW]= m˙⁢ Δhs⁢ ηs⁢ ηmech
ṁ
mass flow, kg/s
ηs
isentropic efficiency — typically 0.80 to 0.88 for a radial inflow turboexpander
ηmech
mechanical efficiency, typically 0.97 to 0.99

Outlet temperature

T2= T1 [1− ηs (1− (p2p1) k−1k ) ]

Worked example — hot gas expander.

FCC flue gas: 60 kg/s at 690 °C (963 K) and 2.8 bar a, expanding to 1.1 bar a. Take cp = 1.12 kJ/(kg·K), k = 1.35, ηs = 0.85.

(p₂/p₁)(k−1)/k = (0.3929)0.2593 = 0.7849

Δhs = 1.12 × 963 × (1 − 0.7849) = 232.0 kJ/kg

P = 60 × 232.0 × 0.85 × 0.98 ≈ 11 600 kW

T₂ = 963 × [1 − 0.85(1 − 0.7849)] = 963 × 0.8171 = 787 K = 514 °C

Check for condensation across the expansion. In cryogenic service the expansion path may cross into the two-phase region. Modest liquid fraction at the outlet is tolerated by many designs — commonly stated limits are of the order of a few per cent by mass — but it must be deliberate, within the vendor's limit, and verified on a Mollier or T–s diagram for the actual composition. Liquid at the inlet or in excess at the outlet erodes the wheel.

6.3Low temperature expanders

Radial inflow turboexpanders, almost universally, running at high speed on a single overhung wheel.

Table 6.3-1 — Cryogenic turboexpander applications
ProcessRole
NGL / LPG recoveryGenerates the cold that condenses C₂+ from the gas stream. Turboexpander plants displaced JT plants for exactly this reason.
Natural gas dew point controlHydrocarbon dew pointing before sales gas delivery
LNG liquefactionNitrogen or methane expander cycles
Air separationRefrigeration for the cold box
Ethylene plantsDemethaniser refrigeration
Helium and hydrogen liquefactionDeep cryogenic refrigeration
Table 6.3-2 — Design features and why they exist
FeatureReason
Variable inlet guide vanesCapacity and efficiency control over a wide flow range; also the primary speed control element
Very high speedSmall wheel, high tip speed. 10 000 to 60 000 rpm is normal; small machines run faster.
Magnetic or gas bearings (optional)Eliminates oil, and with it the risk of oil entering the cold box
Dry gas or labyrinth seals with seal gasPrevents lube oil migration into the process. Seal gas is usually warm, dry process gas or nitrogen.
Cold box integrationMachine is insulated within or adjacent to the cold box; piping arranged for differential contraction
Austenitic stainless / aluminium constructionToughness at cryogenic temperature
Seal gas and lube oil are the usual failure path. Loss of seal gas differential allows lube oil to migrate to the cold end, where it freezes and plugs the cold box — an expensive, slow recovery. Seal gas supply reliability, differential pressure monitoring and a trip on low differential are essential, not optional.

6.4High temperature expanders

Also called hot gas expanders or power recovery turbines. Usually axial, and usually large.

Table 6.4-1 — Hot gas expander applications
ApplicationInlet conditionsNotes
FCC flue gas power recovery620 – 730 °C, 2 – 3.5 bar aThe classic case. Recovers a large fraction of the main air blower power.
Nitric acid tail gas300 – 650 °CDrives the process air compressor
Blast furnace top gas (TRT)Ambient to 250 °CHigh flow, low pressure
Natural gas let-downPre-heated to avoid hydrate / low outlet temperatureCity gate stations, inlet to distribution
Table 6.4-2 — Dominant design problems
ProblemConsequenceMitigation
Catalyst fines erosionFCC flue gas carries alumina/silica catalyst. Blade leading edges and the nozzle ring erode.Third-stage separator upstream, with specified particle size and loading limits; erosion-resistant blade coatings; wide blade spacing
Catalyst depositionFines build up on blades, unbalancing the rotorOn-line water or steam washing; vibration monitoring
High temperature creepBlade and disc lifeNickel-based superalloy blades, disc cooling, temperature limit protection
Thermal transientsDifferential expansion, rubbing, low-cycle fatigueControlled start-up and shutdown ramp rates; hot standby arrangements
Overspeed on load lossTrain accelerates rapidly if the load is lostFast-acting trip and shut-off valve; independent overspeed protection
Erosion sets the maintenance interval, not hours run. Hot gas expander availability is governed almost entirely by how well the upstream separation performs. A third-stage separator operating off-design puts fines into the machine and turns a four-year run into a one-year run. Particulate loading limits in the vendor's specification are hard limits, not targets.

6.5Loading arrangements

The recovered work must go somewhere. The choice affects the whole train layout and control philosophy.

Table 6.5-1 — Expander loading arrangements
ArrangementDescriptionComment
Expander-compressor (compander)Expander wheel and a booster compressor wheel on a common shaftThe standard cryogenic arrangement. Recompresses the residue gas. Self-contained, no external power.
Expander-generatorDrives a generator, directly or through a gearboxExports electrical power. Needs speed control synchronised to the grid.
Integrated trainExpander, motor-generator, steam turbine and main air blower on one shaftFCC power recovery trains. Motor-generator makes up the difference between recovered and required power either way.
Oil / hydraulic brakeWork dissipatedOnly where cold is the objective and power recovery is not worth the equipment
Applicable standard. API 617 Part 4 covers expander-compressors. General-purpose and power-recovery expanders are frequently supplied to the vendor's standard with the project adding API-equivalent requirements for materials, testing, instrumentation (API 670) and lube systems (API 614). Confirm early which standard actually governs — it is often left ambiguous in the inquiry.

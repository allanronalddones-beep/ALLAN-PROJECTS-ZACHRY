1.3Formulas

1.3.1Temperature

Absolute and relative scales

T[K]=t[°C]+273.15
t[°F]= 95⁢t[°C]+32 t[°C]= 59(t[°F]−32)
T[°R]=t[°F]+459.67 T[°R]=95⁢T[K]

Temperature differences

A difference converts by ratio only — the offset does not apply:

1K= 1°C= 1.8°F= 1.8°R
Common error. A 10 °C temperature rise across a bearing is an 18 °F rise, not 50 °F. Apply the +32 offset to temperatures, never to temperature rises, approach temperatures or LMTDs.

1.3.2Viscosity

Dynamic and kinematic viscosity

ν=μρ
ν
kinematic viscosity, m²/s
μ
dynamic (absolute) viscosity, Pa·s
ρ
density, kg/m³

In the practical units used on pump datasheets, this reduces to:

ν[cSt]= μ[cP] SG
Which one does the pump care about? Hydraulic performance correction (head, flow and efficiency de-rating per the Hydraulic Institute method) is driven by kinematic viscosity in cSt. Bearing and seal calculations generally use dynamic viscosity. Check which the vendor has quoted before applying a correction factor.

Saybolt Universal Seconds (SUS / SSU)

For kinematic viscosity above roughly 50 cSt the relationship is effectively linear:

νSUS,100°F ≈4.632⁢ν[cSt] νSUS,210°F ≈4.664⁢ν[cSt]

Below 50 cSt the relationship is non-linear and the ASTM D2161 tables or the full correlation should be used. Indicative values:

1.3.3Specific gravity and API gravity

Specific gravity

SG= ρliquid ρwater

SG is dimensionless and only meaningful with its reference temperatures stated. The petroleum convention is 60/60 °F — liquid at 60 °F referred to water at 60 °F. ISO practice is 15/4 °C. Mixing the two introduces an error of about 0.1 %.

API gravity

°API= 141.5SG60/60 −131.5 SG60/60= 141.5°API+131.5

Water is 10 °API by definition. Higher °API means lighter crude; above 10 °API the liquid floats on water.

1.3.5Flow (liquid)

Mass flow to volumetric flow

Q[m3/h]= W[kg/h] ρ[kg/m3] Q[m3/h]= W[t/h] SG

The second form is the one worth memorising: at SG = 1, one tonne per hour is one cubic metre per hour.

Velocity in a pipe

v= QA = 4⁢Q π⁢d2

In working units, with Q in m³/h and inside diameter d in mm:

v[m/s]= 353.7⁢Q[m3/h] (d[mm])2

Reynolds number

Re= ρ⁢v⁢d μ = v⁢d ν

Laminar below about 2300; fully turbulent above about 4000. Pump suction piping in viscous service is frequently laminar — check before applying a turbulent friction correlation.

1.3.6Head (liquid)

Pressure to head

H= p ρ⁢g

In the units actually used on a pump datasheet:

H[m]= 10.197⁢Δp[bar] SG H[ft]= 2.3067⁢Δp[psi] SG
Head is the property of the pump; pressure is not. A centrifugal pump develops the same head regardless of the liquid’s density — the differential pressure scales with SG. This is why performance curves are always plotted in metres or feet of liquid, and why a pump tested on water will deliver proportionally less differential pressure on a light hydrocarbon.

Net Positive Suction Head available

NPSHa= ps−pv ρ⁢g +Hs −Hf
ps
absolute pressure on the liquid surface in the suction vessel, Pa
pv
vapour pressure of the liquid at pumping temperature, Pa (absolute)
Hs
static head — liquid level above pump centreline, m (negative for a suction lift)
Hf
friction and fitting losses in the suction line, m
Margin, not equality. NPSHa must exceed NPSHr by a margin, not merely equal it. API 610 requires a margin over the vendor’s 3 % head-drop NPSHr; a boiling-point liquid at its bubble point has ps = pv, so the entire available NPSH comes from static head less friction loss.

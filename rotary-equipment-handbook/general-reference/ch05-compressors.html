5.1Types and coverage

Table 5.1-1 — Compressor types compared
TypePrincipleFlowRatio per stageCharacteristics
ReciprocatingPositive displacement, piston in cylinderLow to moderate2 – 4Highest pressures. Pulsating flow. High maintenance. Efficient at high ratio.
Screw — oil floodedPositive displacement, meshing rotorsLow to moderateup to ~20Tolerant of liquid. Oil separation needed. Compact.
Screw — dryPositive displacement, timing gearsLow to moderate3 – 4Oil-free gas. Higher speed and noise.
CentrifugalDynamic, radial impellerModerate to high1.2 – 3 per impellerSmooth flow, high reliability, low maintenance. Surge limits turndown.
AxialDynamic, bladed rotor and statorVery high1.05 – 1.2 per stageHighest efficiency and flow, narrow operating range.
Fan / blowerDynamicVery high< 1.2Low pressure rise; density change usually neglected
The deciding questions, in order. (1) What inlet volume flow? That sets the family. (2) What pressure ratio and discharge pressure? That decides within the family and sets the stage count. (3) What molecular weight? Low MW gases — hydrogen, helium — need very high tip speeds or many stages in a centrifugal, and often push the selection to reciprocating. (4) Is oil-free gas required? (5) Does the gas carry liquid or solids?
Molecular weight dominates centrifugal selection. Head developed per impeller is proportional to tip speed squared and inversely proportional to molecular weight. A compressor that makes a 3:1 ratio in propane (MW 44) may need three times the stages for the same ratio in hydrogen-rich gas (MW 4). Changes to feed composition after the compressor is bought are a classic source of shortfall.

Definitions

The boundary is a convention, not physics. Different standards and companies draw the fan/blower line at different pressures. What matters in practice is whether the density change through the machine is large enough to need accounting for. Below about 7 % rise it is not.

Fan types

Table 5.2-2 — Centrifugal fan blade types
BladeEfficiencyPower curveApplication
Backward curved / airfoil80 – 90 %Non-overloading — power peaks then fallsClean air, high efficiency duty. The default industrial choice.
Radial / paddle60 – 70 %Rises continuouslyDust, particulate, material handling. Rugged, easy to clean.

Forward-curved fans overload. Power rises continuously with flow, so a fan selected for a dirty-filter condition will overload the motor once the filter is cleaned and resistance falls. Backward-curved and airfoil blades reach a power maximum and then decline — this is the meaning of "non-overloading", and it is a reliability argument as much as an efficiency one.

Fan laws and power

The fan laws are the affinity laws of §4.3, with one addition: pressure and power also scale directly with gas density.

Q2Q1 =N2N1 p2p1 = (N2N1)2 ρ2ρ1 P2P1 = (N2N1)3 ρ2ρ1
Pshaft[kW] = Q[m3/s]⁢Δp[Pa] 1000⁢η
Density is where fan calculations go wrong. Fan curves are published at standard air density, 1.2 kg/m³. A forced-draught fan on hot gas, or any fan at altitude, moves the same volume but develops proportionally less pressure and absorbs proportionally less power. Correct both before selecting the motor — and check the cold start case, where dense ambient air can overload a motor sized for hot operation.

Discharge temperature usually sets the stage count, not head. Typical limits: 150 °C for most hydrocarbon service, lower where polymerisation or fouling is a risk, and often 135 °C where oxygen is present. When the calculated T₂ exceeds the limit, an intercooler and another stage are required regardless of whether a single stage could make the head.

5.4Centrifugal compressors

Configurations

Table 5.4-1 — Centrifugal compressor casing arrangements
ArrangementDescriptionApplication
Horizontally splitCasing splits on the centreline; rotor lifted out verticallyLower pressure service. Simple maintenance access.
Vertically split (barrel)Cylindrical outer casing with removable inner bundleHigh pressure, and mandatory for hydrogen-rich gas above defined partial pressure
Integrally gearedMultiple pinions on a bull gear, each with one or two impellersAir separation, plant air, process service with intercooling between every stage. High efficiency.
Overhung single stageImpeller cantileveredLow ratio, high flow — pipeline booster, blower duty
Why hydrogen forces a barrel casing. A horizontally split casing seals along a bolted joint the full length of the machine. Hydrogen's small molecule leaks through that joint. API 617 requires a barrel design above a specified hydrogen partial pressure, where the only static seal is a circumferential head joint.

Surge

Below a minimum flow, the impeller can no longer sustain the pressure imposed on it. Flow reverses, pressure collapses, flow re-establishes, and the cycle repeats several times per second.

Table 5.4-2 — Operating limits of a centrifugal compressor
LimitCauseEffect
Surge (left-hand limit)Flow too low for the head being developedViolent flow reversal. Axial thrust reversal, seal and bearing damage, blade failure. Damaging within seconds.
Stonewall / choke (right-hand limit)Sonic velocity reached at an internal throatHead falls vertically; no further flow possible. Inefficient but not immediately destructive.
Maximum continuous speedMechanical and rotordynamic limitsAPI 617: MCS is 105 % of rated speed
Minimum speedRotordynamics and lube systemVendor-defined
Anti-surge protection is a control system, not a setting. A surge control line is drawn with margin to the left of the surge limit line — typically 10 % in flow. The anti-surge valve must be fast-opening, sized for full recycle, and must fail open. Hot-gas recycle needs a cooler, or successive recycles walk the suction temperature up and move the surge line with it.

5.5Reciprocating compressors

Volumetric efficiency

Gas trapped in the clearance volume re-expands before the suction valve can open, so the cylinder never fills completely.

VE=100−L− C[ Z1Z2 r1k −1 ]
VE
volumetric efficiency, %
L
allowance for valve and ring leakage, typically 3–5 %
C
clearance volume as a percentage of swept volume
r
pressure ratio p₂/p₁, absolute
k
specific heat ratio
Z₁, Z₂
compressibility at suction and discharge
Clearance is also the capacity control. Because VE falls as clearance rises, adding clearance deliberately — fixed pockets, variable-volume clearance pockets — reduces capacity with far less energy waste than recycling. Combined with valve unloaders this gives step and continuous turndown. It is the main reason a reciprocating compressor turns down efficiently.

Piston displacement

PD= π⁢D24 ⁢S⁢N (single acting)

For a double-acting cylinder the crank-end displacement is reduced by the piston rod area:

PD= [ 2⁢π⁢D24 − π⁢d24 ] ⁢S⁢N
D
cylinder bore
d
piston rod diameter
S
stroke
N

Actual inlet capacity is then PD × VE / 100.

Rod load

Rod load is the limiting mechanical parameter, and it has two forms. Gas rod load is the pressure differential across the piston times its area. Combined rod load adds inertial forces from the reciprocating mass. Both have vendor limits that must not be exceeded at any operating case, including start-up, settle-out and upset. Rod reversal matters just as much: the load must change sign each revolution for long enough to let lubricant into the crosshead pin bearing. A non-reversing rod load destroys the bearing even when the magnitude is within limit.

Pulsation and foundation

Table 5.5-1 — API 618 pulsation study approaches
ApproachMethodWhen
1Empirical sizing of pulsation dampenersSmall, low-power, non-critical machines
2Acoustic simulation of the piping systemMost process machines — the common requirement
3Acoustic simulation plus mechanical response analysis of the piping and supportsLarge, high-power or critical machines
Foundation. Reciprocating compressors transmit unbalanced primary and secondary forces and couples into the foundation. The foundation block is normally mass-designed so its natural frequencies are well clear of running speed and its harmonics, with a mass ratio to the machine commonly in the range of 3 to 5 for a block foundation. Measured foundation vibration limits are set by the vendor and the project; ISO 10816-6 covers reciprocating machine vibration evaluation.

Built-in volume ratio

A screw compressor has a fixed geometric volume ratio Vi — the port opens at a predetermined point in the compression regardless of the system pressure.

pinternal= p1⁢ Vik
ConditionNameConsequence
pinternal = pdischargeMatchedOptimum efficiency
pinternal < pdischargeUnder-compressionBack-flow at port opening; lost work
pinternal > pdischargeOver-compressionGas compressed beyond need, then blown down; lost work
Vi must match the duty. A screw compressor is most efficient at one pressure ratio. Operating far from it carries a real energy penalty even though the machine runs happily. Where the ratio varies widely, specify a variable Vi slide valve. Capacity control is normally by slide valve (continuous, roughly 100 % down to 10–25 %) or variable speed.

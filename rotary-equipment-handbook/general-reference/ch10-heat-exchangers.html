10.2TEMA designation

Put the fouling fluid in the tubes, and think before choosing a U-tube. Tube bores can be mechanically cleaned; U-bends cannot. A BEU exchanger with a fouling tube-side fluid cannot be cleaned without chemical circulation, and is a recurring operational complaint. Choose U-tubes when the tube side is clean and differential expansion must be accommodated cheaply.

10.3TEMA classes

Table 10.3-1 — TEMA mechanical standards classes
ClassIntended serviceRelative robustness
RSevere requirements of petroleum and related processingHeaviest — thicker tubesheets, larger corrosion allowance, tighter tolerances
CGenerally moderate requirements of commercial and general process serviceLightest, most economical
BChemical process serviceBetween R and C
Class R for hydrocarbon service. Refinery and oil & gas exchangers are normally TEMA Class R. The class affects corrosion allowance, minimum tube pitch, tubesheet thickness and baffle spacing — it is not just a documentation label, and substituting Class C to save cost changes the physical exchanger.

10.4Thermal calculation

Duty

Q= m˙⁢ cp⁢ ΔT Q= m˙⁢ λ (latent duty)
Q
heat duty, W
ṁ
mass flow, kg/s
cp
specific heat, J/(kg·K)
λ
latent heat of vaporisation or condensation, J/kg

Log mean temperature difference

ΔTlm= ΔT1−ΔT2 ln⁡(ΔT1ΔT2)

Where ΔT1 and ΔT2 are the temperature differences between the two streams at each end of the exchanger, evaluated for pure counter-current flow.

Required area

Q= U⁢A⁢F⁢ ΔTlm A= QU⁢F⁢ΔTlm
U
overall heat transfer coefficient, W/(m²·K)
A
heat transfer area, m²
F
LMTD correction factor for non-counter-current flow — 1.0 for true counter-current or pure isothermal duty
Keep F above about 0.8. A multipass exchanger is not truly counter-current, and F corrects for that. Below roughly 0.8 the factor becomes very sensitive to small changes in terminal temperatures, so a minor shift in operating conditions produces a large change in capability. The usual remedies are more shells in series, or an F-shell. A design sitting at F = 0.7 is fragile.

Worked example — lube oil cooler.

Oil cooled 70 °C → 45 °C; cooling water 32 °C → 40 °C, counter-current.

ΔT₁ = 70 − 40 = 30 K    ΔT₂ = 45 − 32 = 13 K

ΔTlm = (30 − 13) / ln(30/13) = 17 / 0.8362 = 20.3 K

Duty: 12 kg/s of oil, cp = 2.1 kJ/(kg·K) → Q = 12 × 2100 × 25 = 630 kW

With U = 400 W/(m²·K) and F = 0.9: A = 630 000 / (400 × 0.9 × 20.3) = 86 m²

The smallest coefficient controls. Resistances add. If the gas-side film coefficient is 50 W/(m²·K) and the water side is 5000, improving the water side is almost pointless — the gas side is 99 % of the resistance. This is why gas and air services use extended surface (fins): the only effective move is to add area where the resistance is.

Over-specifying fouling makes fouling worse. Generous fouling allowances enlarge the exchanger. A larger exchanger runs at lower velocity for the same flow, and low velocity promotes deposition. The oversized exchanger then fouls faster than the correctly sized one would have, and the clean-condition performance is so far from design that control becomes difficult. Specify fouling from operating experience with the actual fluid, not from the top of a range.
Velocity is the main fouling control. Cooling water in tubes is normally kept above about 1 m/s to limit deposition and biological growth, and below about 2.5 to 3 m/s to limit erosion — lower for copper alloys, higher for titanium and super duplex. Both limits exist and both matter.

Hot air recirculation is the usual cause of underperformance. Exhausted hot air drawn back into the inlet raises the effective ambient, sometimes by several degrees, and the cooler never reaches design duty. Bank spacing, elevation, prevailing wind and adjacent structures all matter. It is a plot layout problem that shows up as a thermal performance problem.

Plate exchangers are the right answer for cooling water duty on a machine package. Lube oil coolers, seal coolers and jacket water coolers are typically low-pressure, moderate-temperature, water-cooled duties with limited plot space — the exact envelope where a plate unit is compact, efficient and cheap. The usual objection is gasket compatibility with the oil; confirm the gasket elastomer against §3.6.

Table 10.9-1 — Where exchangers appear on a machine package

Surface condenserShell and tubeHEI standardsAir removal, vacuum integrity, cooling water fouling
Oil must be on the shell side, water in the tubes. Cooling water is the fouling, corrosive stream and needs to be cleanable, so it belongs in the tubes. The usual arrangement also keeps oil pressure above water pressure, so that a tube leak drives oil into the water rather than water into the lube oil system. Water in the lube oil destroys bearings; the leak direction is a design decision, not an accident.

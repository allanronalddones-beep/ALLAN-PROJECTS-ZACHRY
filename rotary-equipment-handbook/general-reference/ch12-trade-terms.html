12.1What an Incoterm does and does not do

An Incoterm does defineAn Incoterm does not define
Who arranges and pays for carriagePrice, payment terms or currency
Who arranges and pays for insuranceWhen title (ownership) passes
Where risk of loss or damage transfersRemedies for breach of contract
Who handles export and import clearanceGoverning law or dispute resolution
Who pays duties and taxesInspection, warranty or acceptance
Who provides which documentsForce majeure
Risk transfer and title transfer are different events. Incoterms deal only with risk. Title passes according to the sale contract and the applicable law. A buyer can be carrying the risk of damage to equipment it does not yet own. Both must be addressed — the Incoterm handles one of them.

Do not use FOB, CFR or CIF for containerised cargo. These rules place risk transfer at the ship's rail / on board. Containerised equipment is handed to the carrier at a container terminal days before loading, leaving an uninsured gap where nobody is clearly responsible. Use FCA, CPT or CIP for containers — they transfer risk at handover to the carrier, which matches what actually happens. Most packaged rotating equipment ships in containers or as breakbulk through a terminal; FOB remains very widely and wrongly used for both.

12.3Transfer of risk and cost

The C-terms are the ones that catch people out. Under CPT, CIP, CFR and CIF the seller arranges and pays for carriage to the destination — but risk passes at origin. If the compressor is damaged mid-ocean on a CIF contract, it is the buyer's loss even though the seller booked and paid for the ship. This split between the cost point and the risk point is the single most misunderstood feature of Incoterms.

12.4Insurance

Table 12.4-1 — Insurance obligations
RuleSeller's insurance obligationDefault cover level
CIPRequiredInstitute Cargo Clauses (A) — all risks
CIFRequiredInstitute Cargo Clauses (C) — limited, named perils
All other rulesNoneWhichever party bears risk should insure its own exposure
CIF's default insurance is minimal. Institute Cargo Clauses (C) covers a short list of named perils — it does not cover ordinary handling damage, theft or water damage. For a multi-million-peso compressor train this is nowhere near adequate. Either specify ICC (A) explicitly in the contract or use CIP, where ICC (A) is the default. Minimum cover under both is 110 % of contract value in the contract currency.

12.5Changes from Incoterms 2010

Table 12.5-1 — What changed in the 2020 revision
ChangeDetail
DAT became DPU"Delivered at Terminal" was renamed "Delivered at Place Unloaded" — the destination no longer has to be a terminal. DPU is the only rule requiring the seller to unload.
CIP insurance raisedCIP now defaults to ICC (A) all-risks cover. CIF remains at ICC (C).
FCA on-board bill of ladingThe parties may agree that the buyer instructs the carrier to issue an on-board bill of lading to the seller — resolving a long-standing problem with letters of credit under FCA.
Own means of transportFCA, DAP, DPU and DDP now recognise that the buyer or seller may carry the goods in its own vehicles rather than engaging a third-party carrier.
Security requirementsSecurity-related obligations and costs are allocated explicitly.
Cost allocation consolidatedAll costs for each rule are now listed together in article A9/B9.
Earlier versions remain valid if cited. Incoterms editions do not expire. A contract may still invoke Incoterms 2010, and some long-form framework agreements do. That is precisely why the edition must always be stated — "FCA Manila, Incoterms® 2020". Writing "FCA Manila" alone leaves which edition applies open to argument.

12.6Practical guidance for equipment procurement

Table 12.6-1 — Choosing a rule for a machinery purchase
SituationSuggested ruleReasoning
Buyer has a freight forwarder and good logistics capabilityFCABuyer controls routing and cost; clean risk transfer at the vendor's works or a named place
Buyer wants the vendor to manage shipping but keep control of insuranceCPTVendor arranges carriage; buyer insures its own risk from origin
Buyer wants door-to-door with minimum involvementDAP or DPURisk stays with the vendor to site. DPU where the vendor should also unload.
Heavy lift requiring specialist unloading at siteDAP, not DPUThe buyer normally controls site lifting and crane arrangements — do not oblige the vendor to unload into a site it does not control
Buyer does not want to handle import clearanceDDPVendor handles everything, but many vendors will not accept DDP into an unfamiliar jurisdiction
Vendor insists on EXWPush back to FCAUnder EXW the buyer is technically responsible for export clearance in the vendor's own country, which it often legally cannot perform
Table 12.6-2 — Points to settle alongside the Incoterm
ItemWhy
Named place, precisely"DAP Site" is ambiguous. "DAP Batangas Plant, Gate 2, Incoterms® 2020" is not.
Preservation and export packingRotating equipment needs nitrogen blanketing, desiccant, shaft locking and VCI protection. Specify it; the Incoterm does not.
Storage and preservation after deliveryLong site storage before installation needs a documented preservation regime and periodic shaft rotation.

Title and payment milestonesSeparate from risk — must be addressed in the contract
Shock and tilt indicators are worth their cost. For a critical machine, shock recorders and tilt indicators on the transport frame establish whether a machine was mishandled before it reaches site. Without them, a bearing damaged in transit is discovered at commissioning, long after the claim window has closed and when nobody can prove where it happened.

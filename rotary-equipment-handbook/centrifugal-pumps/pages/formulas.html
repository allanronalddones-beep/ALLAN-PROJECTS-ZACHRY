Liquid properties

Specific gravity = ρliquid ⁄ ρwater @ 4 °C   (ρwater = 1000 kg/m³)

Kinematic viscosity ν = μ ⁄ ρ   [m²/s]

10⁴ St = 1 m²/s. Water ≈ 1 cSt. Apply viscosity corrections above about 2 cP.

Head, flow and power

Differential head H = (Pd − Ps) × 10 ⁄ ρ   [m; P in kg/cm², ρ = SG]

Suction / discharge head h = P × 10 ⁄ ρ   [m]

Volumetric flow Q = M ⁄ δ   [m³/h; M in kg/h, δ in kg/m³]

Hydraulic power PH = (Q × ρ × g × H) ⁄ (3.6 × 10⁶)   [kW; Q in m³/h, ρ in kg/m³]

Shaft power PS = PH ⁄ ηp

Motor power at coupling PM = (√3 × V × I × cos φ × ηe) ⁄ 1000   [kW]

Pump efficiency ηp = PH ⁄ PM

ηp = hydraulic × volumetric × mechanical efficiency. Calculator →

System resistance

Ht = Hst + k·Q²   k = Hf,design ⁄ Qdesign²

Velocity v = Q ⁄ A  ·  A = π D² ⁄ 4  ·  velocity head = v² ⁄ 2g

Static head is flow-independent; friction head goes with Q². Calculator →

Affinity laws and corrections

Q₂ = Q₁ × (N₂⁄N₁)  ·  H₂ = H₁ × (N₂⁄N₁)²  ·  P₂ = P₁ × (N₂⁄N₁)³

By analogy for diameter: Q₂ = Q₁ × (D₂⁄D₁)  ·  H₂ = H₁ × (D₂⁄D₁)²  ·  P₂ = P₁ × (D₂⁄D₁)³

Trim correction: A = 16.2 + 0.838 × C   [%, A = actual, C = calculated]

KSB approximation: D₂⁄D₁ ≈ √(Q₂⁄Q₁) ≈ √(H₂⁄H₁)

Viscosity: Qvis = CQQw · Hvis = CHHw · ηvis = Cηηw

Valid only at constant efficiency. Divergence becomes significant beyond ±25% speed change. C coefficients from the Hydraulic Institute chart. Calculator →

NPSH and cavitation

NPSH-a = Ha + Hg ± Hst − Hf − Hvap   [m]

Head from pressure: H = P ⁄ (ρ × g)  ·  Ha ≈ 10.33 ⁄ SG  ·  Hvac = −(mm Hg ⁄ 1000) × 13.6 ⁄ SG

NPSH-r = K₁·CM1²⁄2g + K₂·W₁²⁄2g

First term = friction and acceleration losses (dominant at low suction energy); second = blade entry losses (dominant at high suction energy). By convention NPSH-a already includes the vapour pressure correction, so the requirement is simply NPSH-a > NPSH-r. Calculator →

Curve stability and steepness

Steepness % = (HSO − HBEP) ⁄ HBEP × 100

Combined parallel efficiency: η = [H(Q₁+Q₂) × 100] ⁄ [K(P₁+P₂)]

Small ΔH/ΔQ = flat (firewater duty); large ΔH/ΔQ = steep. Combined NPSH-r in parallel = the largest of the pumps.

Speed, torque and acceleration

Pump N–T characteristic: T = k·N²

Full load torque T = (30 × P) ⁄ (π × N)   [kN·m; P in kW, N in rpm]

Accelerating torque Tacc = (Wk² × ΔN) ⁄ (308 × Δt)   [lb·ft, FPS units]

Acceleration time t = (Wk² ⁄ 308) × [ΔN₁⁄T₁ + ΔN₂⁄T₂ + …]

Torque multipliers from full load: ×0.563 at 75% speed, ×0.25 at 50%, ×0.063 at 25%. Closed-valve torque at full speed: 30–50% of nominal. Wk²EQ is the sum of the train's moments of inertia.

Rotor forces

Momentum force FX = ρ × Q² ⁄ AE   [N; Q in m³/s, A in m²]

Peripheral velocity U = π × D × N ⁄ 60   [m/s]

Radial thrust at shut-off RSO = KSO × (HSO × ρ ⁄ 2.31) × D₂ × B₂   [lb]

At other points R = RSO × (K⁄KSO) × (H⁄HSO),  K = KSO[1 − (Q⁄QBEP)x]

Approximate: R = K × (H × ρ ⁄ 2.31) × D₂ × B₂,  K = 0.36[1 − (Q⁄QBEP)²]

Bearing reactions: RB = Fr(A+B)⁄B · RA = Fr − RB · PB = X·RB + Y·Fax

FPS for the radial thrust formulae: H in ft, D₂ and B₂ in inches, Q in gpm. Exponent x: 0.7 at Ns 500 rising linearly to 3.3 at Ns 3500. KSO from the chart against Ns, differing for open and closed impellers.

Shaft deflection δ ∝ L³ ⁄ D⁴

Pressure on shrouds assumed = ¾ × PD  ·  Area A = πD²⁄4

Vane pass frequency = number of impeller vanes × running speed

Do not use L³/D⁴ to compare pumps between vendors — ask for the OEM's actual deflection values at the most severe flow condition. Balancing hole pressure relief: region under the back ring falls to about 2–10% of differential pressure.

Overhaul economics

Monthly excess energy cost = excess kW × (hours per month × duty fraction) × unit cost

Linear deterioration rate = (cost at month n) ⁄ n   [$ per month per month]

Optimum: average overhaul cost per month = average excess energy cost per month

Average overhaul cost = Coverhaul ⁄ t  ·  Average excess energy = rate × ½ × t

Solve iteratively for t. Valid only if wear progresses at a uniformly increasing rate. For variable speed pumps the power penalty scales with the cube of the speed ratio, so deterioration is far more expensive and invisible in production terms. Worked example in Chapter 09.

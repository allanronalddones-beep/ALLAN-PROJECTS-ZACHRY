About these calculators

Everything here runs in your browser with JavaScript — no network request is made and nothing you type leaves the machine. The page works from a local copy or a USB stick, which is the point: they are meant to be usable in a plant office with no connectivity.

Each calculator states its units explicitly and carries the derivation it uses. They are engineering aids for checking and screening, not a substitute for the vendor's own calculation — and several of them encode empirical relationships whose limitations are set out in the chapters they link to.

By convention NPSH-a already includes the vapour pressure correction, so you only need NPSH-a > NPSH-r. Note the absolute pressure field: use 101.33 kPa a for an open vessel at sea level, add gauge pressure for a pressurised vessel, or enter the absolute pressure directly for a vacuum vessel. The 3% trap

The NPSH-r read from a curve is the 3% head-drop value. It is not the onset of cavitation and it is not a damage-free value — the NPSH needed to suppress all cavitation is typically 4–5× it, and can range from 2 to 20×. A pump passing the margin check above can still erode its impeller, particularly on cold water and on high suction energy duty.

Q in m³/h, ρ in kg/m³ (= SG × 1000), g = 9.81 m/s², H in metres of liquid column. Motor efficiency is not a fixed number — part-load efficiency is much lower than full-load, so use the manufacturer's efficiency-versus-load table for the actual operating point.

Size the driver on the end of the curve

The "next IEC frame" suggestion above adds only 10% to the entered duty. For a low specific speed pump with an overloading P–Q characteristic, power keeps rising as flow rises — so the driver must be sized on the power at the end of the curve, around 120% of BEP flow, not at the rated point. Otherwise the first time the system resistance drops, the motor trips. See Chapter 05.

Temperature rise at low flow

How long a pump can safely run at closed discharge — which is what sets your minimum-flow bypass requirement and your low-flow trip setting.

Time to limit

TR = (5.1 × HP) ⁄ (Qgal × SH × SG)   [°F/min]

The underlying relation is in FPS units: HP is brake horsepower at shut-off and Qgal is casing volume in US gallons; the calculator converts from the kW and litres you enter. Estimate casing volume geometrically. The equation neglects heat loss through the casing, so the result is conservative.

Choosing the allowable rise

50 °F (28 °C) is the usual maximum. Use less if that would exceed the casing design temperature, raise vapour pressure into a critical NPSH condition, or cause damaging vaporisation at the seal face. Boiler feed water is limited to 15 °F (8 °C).

And the material may set a lower ceiling than flashing does — a polypropylene pump may be limited to 180 °F where the cavitation limit is 210 °F. Mechanical seals, packing, bearings and wear ring tolerances can each dictate a lower figure. Full treatment in Chapter 05.

Static head is flow-independent — level difference plus any vessel pressure difference. Friction head goes with the square of flow, which is why the curve turns up so sharply and why parallel pumping disappoints. Equivalent-length method and a full worked example in Chapter 03.

What these calculators deliberately do not do

Known limitations — stated plainly
  • No viscosity correction. The Hydraulic Institute correction chart is a graphical lookup of CQ, CH and Cη that cannot be reduced to a reliable formula. For fluids above about 2 cP, read the coefficients from the chart and apply them — see Chapter 03.
  • No slurry correction. Q–H curves for solids-laden liquids can only be determined by experiment. There is no general chart.
  • No axial thrust or radial load calculation. Those depend on impeller and casing geometry that only the OEM has, several of the governing variables are indeterminate, and the results would be approximate at best. Where accurate values matter, they are measured.
  • No friction loss calculation from pipe geometry. Enter the friction head you have computed from pipe friction tables and equivalent-length nomograms; the system head calculator takes it as an input rather than pretending to derive it.
  • No KSO thrust factor lookup. That is a chart read against specific speed, differing for open and closed impellers.

Where a calculation is not here, it is because reducing it to a formula would give a number with false authority. The formula and data sheet carries every equation in this knowledge base, including those that need a chart or a vendor input to complete.

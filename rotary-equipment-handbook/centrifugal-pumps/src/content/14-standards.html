Which standard governs which decision

All editions must be verified

Standards are revised continuously. This page cites the editions referenced in the source material — API 610 7th and 8th editions, API 610 6th edition for the high-energy definition, NACE MR-01-75, and contemporaneous Hydraulic Institute and ANSI practice. Several have been superseded. Table numbers, tolerance bands, clearance tables and acceptance criteria all move between editions.

Use this page to know which standard to consult and what it governs. Take the actual numbers from the current edition in force on your project.

Choosing between API, ANSI/ASME and ISO

The decision rule

Service criticality decides the standard, not habit and not the engineer's comfort. Run the criticality assessment first.

Selection guidance
ServiceStandardReasoning
Critical refinery pump on hazardous hydrocarbons; no standby; high-energy duty API 610 The premium is justified by the consequences of failure and the cost of downtime.
Ordinary chemical or general process duty ANSI/ASME B73.1 Ordinary applications do not need the full API feature set, and the premium is not justified. Dimensional interchangeability between brands is a real operational advantage for spares and replacement.
Firewater NFPA 20 Purpose-written for the duty, including the flat-curve requirement that firewater headers need.
Where an ISO basis is specified ISO 5199 + ISO 2858 5199 for design, 2858 for dimensions. 2858 alone fixes nothing about construction.
Both failure modes cost money

Over-specifying buys cost, lead time and spares complexity without buying reliability. Under-specifying a hazardous high-energy hydrocarbon duty to a general-purpose standard is a false economy that gets paid for in incidents and maintenance. Neither is the safe default.

For a comparative study of API, ANSI and ISO, the source material recommends the technical paper ISO-5199 Standard Addresses Today's Reliability Requirements For Chemical Process Pumps by Pierre H. Fabeck (Durco Europe, Brussels) and R. Barry Erickson (The Duriron Company, Dayton, Ohio), presented at the 7th Pumps Symposium, Texas A&M University, 1990.

Scope — what this knowledge base does and does not cover

What it covers

  • Pump classification and the centrifugal construction families, including sealless designs.
  • Component construction in engineering detail: impellers, casings and recuperators, wear rings, shafts and sleeves, packing, mechanical seals, bearing arrangements and isolators, couplings.
  • Hydraulics quantitatively: head, flow, system resistance, efficiency and power, characteristic curves, affinity and viscosity corrections, specific speed, and the full cavitation / recirculation / NPSH treatment.
  • Rotor forces: axial thrust by impeller and rotor arrangement, radial load by casing type, shaft deflection and bearing load resolution.
  • Operating behaviour: curve stability and steepness, velocity triangles, parallel and series operation, abnormal operating quadrants, speed–torque and acceleration, every regulation method ranked by energy cost, and the allowable operating region with its thermal and mechanical limits.
  • The project lifecycle: system analysis and specification, data sheet content, bid request and weighted bid analysis, material and NDE inspection, hydrostatic and performance testing with tolerances, NPSH testing, foundation and grouting, alignment, piping rules, pre-operational checks and start-up.
  • Maintenance: four-pillar condition monitoring, pre-removal diagnosis, strip-down dimensional limits, reassembly of single-stage, vertical and multistage barrel pumps including Gap-A and Gap-B, and the overhaul timing economics.
  • Field tools: a 16-symptom troubleshooting guide with a reverse cause index, six browser-based calculators, and a consolidated printable formula and data sheet.

Authorship, status and how to use this responsibly

Status of this document

This is an internal engineering reference and training aid prepared for Zachry Philippines. It is not a design code, a standard, a project specification, or a substitute for any of them.

It is original reference material, written to cover the engineering subject matter. It is not a reproduction of Practical Centrifugal Pumps or of any other publication, and it contains none of their figures or text. Factual values — clearances, tolerances, material grades, standard requirements — are drawn from the sources listed above and attributed to them.

For any design, procurement, acceptance, repair or operating decision: work from the current edition of the governing standard, the project specification, and the OEM manual for the actual machine. Where this knowledge base conflicts with those documents, those documents govern.

Keeping it current

Values most likely to have moved since the source material was written, and therefore worth checking first against a current standard:

  • API 610 minimum wear-ring clearances and the material class tables (table numbers have changed between editions);
  • API 610 performance test tolerances;
  • the NACE MR-01-75 designation, now MR0175 / ISO 15156;
  • the fire pump standard reference (NFPA 20, not NFPA 21);
  • Hydraulic Institute NPSH margin guidance;
  • ASME Section VIII acceptance appendix numbering.

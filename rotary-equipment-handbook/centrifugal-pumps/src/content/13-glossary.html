Hydraulic terms

Flow, head and performance
BEP
Best Efficiency Point		 The one flow and head combination the pump was designed for, where total losses are minimum and efficiency maximum. Nearly every force and failure mode in a centrifugal pump is minimised at BEP and grows as the duty moves away from it.
Differential head Discharge head minus suction head — what the pump actually produces. Expressed in metres of liquid column, and independent of the liquid at a given operating point.
Shut-off head, HSO Head developed at zero flow with the discharge valve closed. For a pump with a stable curve, the maximum head it can ever develop.
Rated point The flow and head the vendor guarantees, marked by a small triangle on the curve. Usually 5–10% above the normal operating point.
Normal operating point The flow the process actually demands most of the time. The point that should drive selection — specifying only a rated point invites oversizing.
End of the curve Around 120% of BEP flow. The point a low specific speed pump's driver should be sized on, because its power characteristic is overloading.
Static head, Hst The flow-independent component of system head — level difference plus any vessel pressure difference.
Friction head, Hf The flow-dependent component, proportional to Q². Dominating this term is what makes parallel pumping disappoint.
System resistance curve Hst + kQ². The pump's operating point is always where this curve crosses the pump curve — never where you wish it were.
Equivalent length The length of straight pipe producing the same loss at a given flow as a fitting does. The practical way to total up valve, bend and reducer losses.
Iso-efficiency curves Contours of constant efficiency plotted on the Q–H field across the range of available impeller diameters. Parabolic through the origin, because constant specific speed means constant efficiency.
Steepness (HSO − HBEP) / HBEP × 100. Low ΔH/ΔQ is a flat curve (wanted for firewater); high ΔH/ΔQ is steep.
Stable / unstable curve A stable Q–H curve droops continuously, so one head gives one flow. An unstable curve rises to a maximum then falls, so one head gives two possible flows — and the pump oscillates between them.
Overloading / non-overloading Describes the P–Q curve. Overloading (low specific speed) means power rises with flow, so the motor can trip when system resistance drops. Non-overloading (high specific speed) means power is flat or falls.
Affinity laws Q ∝ N, H ∝ N², P ∝ N³. True similarity laws for speed at constant efficiency; applied to diameter only by analogy and approximately.
Allowable operating region The flow band within which rotor forces, temperature rise and recirculation stay inside design limits. Typically 70–120% of BEP.

Suction conditions and cavitation

NPSH and the phenomena it governs
NPSH-a
available		 Net inlet pressure in absolute terms after suction system losses, expressed as head. A property of the installation. By convention it already has the vapour pressure subtracted.
NPSH-r
required		 The NPSH the pump needs. A property of the pump. Industry-standard definition: the NPSH at which first-stage head drops by 3%.
NPSH margin NPSH-a − NPSH-r, normally required to be at least 1.0–1.5 m. Better expressed as a ratio scaled to suction energy.
Cavitation Vapour bubbles forming where local pressure falls to vapour pressure, then imploding as pressure recovers. Damage appears on the leading face of the non-pressure side of the vanes as pockmarking and vane thinning.
Incipient cavitation The actual onset of cavitation — at an NPSH 2 to 20 times the 3% NPSH-r. Causes minimal damage itself; the peak erosion rate sits between the 3% value and the incipient value.
Recirculation Flow reversal at the inlet or outlet tips of the impeller vanes. Produces noise and vibration that imitate cavitation and is routinely misdiagnosed as it. Damages the opposite side of the vane. Cured by operating closer to BEP — not by adding suction head.
Suction energy A qualitative measure rising with suction nozzle size, pump speed, suction specific speed and specific gravity. Low suction energy pumps tolerate thin margins; high suction energy pumps do not.
Breakaway NPSH-r The sharp rise in NPSH-r at high flow, where internal velocities raise losses and static head falls toward vapour pressure. The mechanism behind a pump that cavitates when NPSH-a is increased.
Vapour pressure Pressure exerted by molecules escaping the liquid to form a vapour phase. For a pure liquid it depends on temperature alone — which is why suction line heat gain consumes NPSH-a.
Pre-rotation / pre-whirl Rotation of liquid entering the impeller. Reduces the mismatch between flow angle and blade angle at off-BEP flows. Positive pre-whirl is usable for regulation; negative pre-whirl costs efficiency.
Shockless entry The condition where flow angle θ equals blade inlet angle β, so incidence angle is zero. Occurs at BEP.
Incidence angle ψ = β − θ. Once it exceeds a critical value, flow separates from the vane and a stalled region forms.

Pump numbers

Dimensionless and quasi-dimensionless indices
Specific speed, Ns N√Q / H3/4, with head per stage and no double-suction correction. Indexes impeller type, geometric similarity, attainable efficiency and curve shape. Conventionally in US units (gpm, ft, rpm).
Suction specific speed, Nss N√Q / (NPSH-r)3/4, with Q halved for a double-suction impeller. The higher it is, the narrower the stable operating band. Preferred range 8 000–11 000.
Head coefficient, ψ H / (u₂²/2g). Used with the flow coefficient to build universal characteristics that allow comparison between dissimilar machines — and testing on another fluid.
Flow coefficient, φ Q / (u₂A), where A = πd₂²/4.
L³/D⁴ The shaft slenderness proxy to which deflection is proportional. Widely quoted in bid comparisons and not a valid basis for comparing reliability between pumps — ask for actual deflection values instead.
Modelling law Two geometrically similar pumps against the same head have similar flow conditions if speeds are inversely proportional to size; capacity then varies as the square of size. Used for very large hydroelectric pumps.

Construction

Components and configurations
Impeller eye The inlet opening through which liquid enters the impeller. Single-suction has one; double-suction has two.
Shroud The disc enclosing the impeller vanes. A closed impeller has front and back shrouds; semi-open omits the front; open designs reduce or omit the back shroud too.
Scalloped impeller An open impeller with the back shroud partially (partially scalloped) or almost entirely (fully scalloped) removed to reduce axial thrust.
Vortex / non-clog impeller A fully shrouded open impeller located above the volute that does not energise the liquid directly — it creates a vortex which does. Experiences almost no radial force, so it tolerates extended closed-discharge running.
Recuperator Generic term for whatever converts impeller outlet velocity into pressure — vaneless guide ring, concentric casing, volute, vaned diffuser, diagonal or axial diffuser vanes.
Volute The spiral casing collecting flow from the impeller. Single volutes are most efficient at BEP but carry high radial load away from it; double volutes reduce radial load across the range.
Cutwater / volute tongue The dividing edge where the volute passage begins. Minimises recirculation and reduces radial load — and its clearance to the impeller sets vane pass vibration.
Throat The narrowest section of a volute or diffuser passage, where kinetic energy begins converting to pressure.
Wear ring Sacrificial ring fitted at the impeller eye (impeller ring) and in the casing (case ring) so that running clearance wear and any contact damage is confined to cheap replaceable parts. Deliberately given a hardness differential.
Balancing hole Hole through the impeller back shroud reducing the pressure behind the impeller to near suction pressure, cutting axial thrust. Normally used with a back wear ring.
Back vanes Vanes cast on the impeller back shroud that pump liquid outward, relieving back pressure and axial thrust. Clearance-dependent, so unreliable as it wears, and costs up to 3% efficiency.
Balancing drum / piston A rotating disk running at close clearance against a stationary ring in a stacked-impeller multistage pump, with the leakage piped back to suction. Opposes axial thrust at the cost of recirculation loss.
Repeller An auxiliary impeller with radial blades on its back shroud that reduces the pressure in front of the packing. Used for toxic or corrosive service.
Lantern ring A circumferentially drilled ring replacing one packing ring, admitting clean sealing liquid into the packing set. Used for dirty or high-pressure liquid.
Throat bush A restrictive bush at the bottom of the stuffing box or seal housing, used where discharge pressure is high. Often the first thing a deflecting shaft contacts on a pump without wear rings.
Shaft sleeve Sacrificial sleeve fitted over the shaft so packing or seals wear the sleeve, not the shaft. The secondary-seal area is hard coated against fretting.
Primary / secondary seal In a mechanical seal, the primary seal is the pair of lapped mating faces. The secondary seals (O-ring, wedge, V-ring, bellows) seal the static joints and must be partially dynamic so the primary ring can creep forward as faces wear.
Cartridge seal A pre-assembled mechanical seal with factory-set seal tension, released by tabs on installation. Removes the skilled seal-setting step from site work — which is why API 682 promotes it.
Seal tension The spring compression setting the face load: F − (S + A + G), typically 3–4 mm on a multi-spring seal.
Light band An interference fringe used to measure seal face flatness. One helium light band = 0.3 µm. Allowable flatness is 3 bands (0.9 µm).
Bearing isolator The end seal protecting the bearing housing from contamination — felt, lip seal, labyrinth or magnetic. Usually the weakest link in bearing life.
Overhung / in-between bearings Rotor support configuration. Overhung cantilevers the impeller beyond the bearings; in-between supports the impellers from both sides. The dominant influence on shaft deflection.
Centreline support Casing support at the shaft axis, so the shaft centreline stays put as the casing grows thermally. API 610 requires it above 177 °C.
Close-coupled / monobloc No coupling: the impeller mounts on an extended motor shaft. No alignment to do, but the motor must come off to reach the impeller.
Sealless pump Canned motor or magnetic drive construction with no dynamic shaft seal — the rotor runs inside a containment shell. Depends on the pumped liquid for cooling and lubrication, so dry running destroys it.
Barrel / double case pump Multistage construction with an inner assembly carrying the hydraulic passages inside an outer pressure-vessel casing. Often supplied with a spare inner subassembly to minimise downtime.

Forces, assembly and condition

Rotor behaviour and overhaul terms
Axial thrust Net axial force from internal pressures on the exposed areas of the rotating element, plus a momentum term. Several governing variables are indeterminate, so calculated values are approximate.
Radial load Force from non-uniform pressure distribution around the impeller. Near zero at BEP in a volute pump, maximum at shut-off — and reversed in sense in a concentric casing.
Shaft deflection Bending under a constant unidirectional load. The deflected shaft rotates about its own bent centreline, reversing stress every revolution → fatigue. Limit at seal faces is 0.05 mm.
Shaft whip A different motion: the shaft end orbits, generating a cone, with the centreline shifting 180° for every 180° of rotation. A rotordynamic problem, not a radial load problem — commonly confused with deflection.
Vane pass frequency Number of impeller vanes × running speed. Excited by too small a gap between impeller OD and cutwater; corrected via Gap-B at no efficiency cost.
Gap-A Radial clearance between impeller side plate ODs and the diffuser channel side plate IDs. Range 35–85 mils, preferred 50; ineffective beyond 125.
Gap-B Radial clearance between impeller vane OD and diffuser or volute vane ID, as a percentage of impeller vane diameter. 4–12% (preferred 6%) for diffuser types, 6–12% (preferred 10%) for volutes; add 4% if both vane counts are even.
Endplay / axial float Axial shaft movement measured with a dial gauge. 0.02–0.07 mm; excess causes pitting and fretting at the packing or seal contact.
Runout Shaft roundness combined with permanent bow, measured on V-blocks. Max 0.05 mm — and because a seal sees it on both sides, 0.075 mm of runout moves the faces 0.15 mm in total.
Soft foot Movement at a machine foot when its hold-down bolt is released, indicating the machine is not sitting flat. Limit 1 mil (0.025 mm).
DBSE Distance Between Shaft Ends. Set with shafts pulled toward each other, or with a sleeve-bearing motor shaft at its magnetic centre.
Laitance The weak, cement-rich surface layer formed when concrete is cast. Must be chipped or blasted off before grouting, or the grout has nothing sound to bond to.
Grout Hardenable material — epoxy or cement-based — placed under and around the baseplate to give a uniform load-bearing surface and damp vibration.
Brinelling Indentation of a bearing raceway from impact loading, typically during installation or transport. Presents as a low-pitch bearing noise.
Galling Adhesive wear between like metals on contact, progressing rapidly toward seizure. The reason wear rings are given a hardness differential and why SS 316 is avoided for them.
Fretting Surface damage from small-amplitude relative motion, typically under a secondary seal on the shaft sleeve. Countered by hard coating that area.
Fugitive emission Process fluid escaping between mechanical seal faces to atmosphere. Where unacceptable, dual seal arrangements are used; a seal at 3 light bands of flatness passes a < 100 ppm specification.
PPM Predictive and Preventive Maintenance — here, the four-pillar programme of performance, vibration, oil/particle and system analysis.
Bad actor A machine that fails repeatedly on the slightest off-duty provocation. Classified as critical regardless of its process duty, because its failure rate makes it one.
Witnessed / observed test API 610 contractual terms. Witnessed applies a hold to production until the purchaser attends. Observed proceeds on schedule, but the vendor may not move to the next step if the purchaser is absent.
Failure Not necessarily a seizure or blowout. A pump has failed when it is no longer capable of performing its specified function under the specified conditions — including drawing more power than specified while running perfectly smoothly.

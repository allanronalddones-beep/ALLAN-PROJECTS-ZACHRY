How to use this knowledge base

The nine core chapters follow a pump through its whole life, in the order the questions actually arrive on a project: what it is, how it is built, how it behaves hydraulically, what forces it carries, how it responds to the system, then specification, testing, installation and maintenance. Read them in sequence for a grounding, or jump straight to the section you need.

The field tools are built for the moment you are standing at the pump or on a call with a vendor. The troubleshooting guide is organised by the symptom someone reports rather than by the component at fault. The calculators run entirely in your browser, so they work on a laptop with no network. The formula sheet is designed to be printed and kept in a site folder.

Press Ctrl K (or /) anywhere to search every page by section.

The ten things that cause most pump trouble

If you read nothing else here, read this. Across specification, installation and operation, the same handful of mistakes account for the large majority of premature pump failures.

Recurring root causes and where they are covered
Root causeWhat it doesWhere
Running far off BEP Radial load, shaft deflection, suction and discharge recirculation, seal and bearing failure. The single most common operating fault. 05
Inadequate NPSH margin Cavitation erosion of the impeller inlet, noise, vibration, lost head. The 3% head-drop NPSH-r is not a damage-free value. 03
Oversizing the pump Generous friction allowances put the duty far right of BEP, then the valve is throttled forever. Wasted energy plus high radial load. 06
Pipe strain on the casing Distorts the casing, destroys alignment, cracks nozzles, kills seals. If you need a lever to make up the flange, the pump is already lost. 08
Bad suction piping Elbow straight onto the nozzle, wrong reducer orientation, insufficient submergence. Non-uniform inlet flow, vortexing, air entrainment. 08
Misalignment and soft foot Vibration, coupling and bearing wear, seal face runout. Final alignment must be re-checked after the piping is bolted up. 08
Contaminated bearing lube 0.002% water in the oil costs about 48% of bearing life; 0.10% costs about 90%. Only about one bearing in ten reaches its design life. 02
Worn wear-ring clearance Recirculation loss, lost efficiency, disturbed impeller inlet flow which raises NPSH-r, and loss of rotor support. 02
High suction specific speed Nss above 11 000 narrows the stable band sharply. Buying NPSH-r cheaply buys a pump that cannot be turned down. 03
Extended low-flow running Casing contents heat up toward flashing, internals expand and seize, radial load and recirculation peak. 05

A note on scope and authority

Read this before using the numbers

This is a reference and training aid, not a design code and not a substitute for the governing standard or the pump manufacturer's data. Clearances, tolerances, material grades and acceptance criteria quoted here are indicative values drawn from the sources listed in Standards & Sources and reflect editions current when those sources were written — API 610, for example, has been revised repeatedly since.

For any real design, procurement, acceptance or repair decision: work from the current edition of the applicable standard, the project specification, and the OEM manual for the actual machine. Where this knowledge base and those documents disagree, they win.
